What the headline number looked like after an honest re-validation.

The symptom predictor was re-run under validation regimes designed to break it: blocked cross-validation with an embargo, feature selection nested inside every training fold, prior-day activity forced into the model, and a permutation null. This page reports what survived and what did not. Production code and canonical data were read-only throughout.

Bottom line

The signal is real but weaker than first reported. Lower nightly RMSSD does track higher self-reported autonomic symptom burden, and that association survives a permutation null (p = 0.003) and adjustment for prior-day activity load.

The optimism came from feature selection, not from time. Nesting the forward selection inside each training fold drops the headline from 0.829 to 0.795. Temporal leakage is negligible here because the target has ≈0 autocorrelation across diary days.

The 48-hour-ahead deployment model does not hold up. Restricted to lag ≥ 2 and validated honestly it lands at ≈0.61 with a permutation p of 0.095 — indistinguishable from chance at any usable threshold. It should not be presented as a forecasting product.

Wording had to change. The target is the diary field disfuncion_autonomica ≥ 5 — a self-report, not a measured autonomic dysfunction. Describing it as "autonomic dysfunction predicted from HRV" overstates the construct.

What each test was trying to break

Reproduction (step 0)

Forward selection + LOO reproduced the production numbers to ±0.0000 on every target, so every delta below is attributable to the validation change and not to harness drift.

Temporal leakage (step 1)

LOO 0.829 versus blocked-embargo 0.823 — a gap of +0.005. The diary target has essentially no autocorrelation across diary days (lag-1 ACF −0.03), so leaving one day out does not leak through its temporal neighbours. The HRV features themselves do persist day to day (lag-1 ≈ 0.22), but a sparse, non-autocorrelated target blunts that into negligible CV leakage.

Feature-selection leakage (step 2)

This is where the optimism lives. Selecting features on the full dataset and then cross-validating reuses the held-out day twice. Nesting selection inside each fold costs the headline 0.034 AUC — and costs the fatigue model 0.152, which is the single largest correction in the audit. hrv_rmssd_night_t0 is the one feature stable across folds (selected in 4 of 5 for the headline, 5 of 5 for severity, PEM and fatigue).

Activity confound (step 3)

The obvious alternative explanation is that HRV is a proxy for how much the subject moved the day before. Forcing prior-day met_minutes into the model leaves the HRV coefficients essentially unchanged (hrv_rmssd_night_t0 −1.22 → −1.27) and costs only 0.018 AUC. For this target the HRV signal is not an activity artifact.

Permutation null (step 4)

Target labels shuffled, full nested pipeline re-run. Observed nested AUC 0.772 against a null mean of 0.495 and a 95th percentile of 0.663 → p = 0.003. The deployment model fails this test (p = 0.095), which is the clearest single reason not to ship it.

Claims corrected as a result

  • "Predicts autonomic dysfunction" → predicts a self-reported autonomic symptom score crossing a threshold. The construct is a diary field, not a measurement.
  • Headline AUC reported as the leakage-safe nested figure, with the optimistic LOO value shown alongside rather than instead of it.
  • The 48-hour-ahead framing is withdrawn from the results narrative. The honest number is 0.614 with p = 0.095.
  • Brain fog is reported with its base rate and PR-AUC attached, because AUC alone is misleading at a 55/6 class split.
